UX case study · Biblely · personal project

Designing a Bible app for people who'd rather read alone.

Biblely explains scripture line by line, in plain words, in Chinese and English. I'm a Christian, and I built it for a problem I have myself. I did every part myself: the interviews, the personas, the flow, every screen, and a coded prototype you can use below.

01 · What I heard

People don't stop reading because they stop believing.

I interviewed my mom, a few friends from church, and myself. Most people in my church are Chinese, but the problem isn't Chinese-specific — people everywhere read the Bible, and they get stuck in the same places. Four findings came up again and again:

#What I heardWhat it meansProduct answer
F1“Every translation reads differently, and none of them are easy.”The text itself is the first barrier — classical Chinese and old English bothPlain-language explanation under every verse
F2“There's no pastor to walk me through it line by line.”Sunday sermons explain; reading alone at home doesn'tA “tutor” voice: chapter summary, key points, verse-by-verse notes
F3“Without a small group I don't keep going.”Consistency comes from accountability, not willpowerProgress, streaks and a reflection at the end of each chapter
F4“I'd rather learn quietly on my own.”Not everyone wants a group — and groups are what most apps pushPrivate by default; sharing is always a choice

The job to be done: “When I can't make it to Sunday service, help me understand today's passage as if someone were explaining it to me.”

02 · Who it's for

Two readers, one app.

Persona 1

The quiet learner

Who
University student in Canada, Christian, reads in both Chinese and English
Context
Classes and work clash with Sunday service; prefers studying alone over group meetings
Needs
Someone to explain the passage without having to join anything; switch languages when a verse doesn't land
Frustration
Apps that are either a wall of text or a social feed
“I want to understand it — I just don't want to do it in a room full of people.”

Persona 2

The first-time reader

Who
Middle-aged or older, Chinese-first, without much formal higher education
Context
Reads at home, often on a phone a family member set up; small text is hard
Needs
Everyday words instead of theology; large type; the option to listen instead of read
Frustration
The Chinese Union Version's classical phrasing; feeling embarrassed to ask
“I read every word and still don't know what it said.”

03 · Flow & structure

From “I'm stuck” to “I get it”, without leaving the page.

Before drawing a single screen I mapped the one path that matters: a reader opens the app alone and finishes a chapter understanding it. Every step answers one of the four findings (F1 to F4). Anything that isn't on this path — community, groups — lives in its own tab, so the reading never pushes anyone into a crowd.

  1. 1Open the appHome resumes where you stopped — verse 15 of 36F3
  2. 2Chapter summaryWhat this chapter is about, in three key pointsF2
  3. 3Verse + plain noteAn everyday-words explanation under every verseF1
  4. 4Comprehension questionsTry first, then reveal the answerF2
  5. 5Private reflectionWritten for yourself; only you can see itF4
  6. 6Chapter completeProgress and streak move forwardF3
At step 3 · stuck on a verse?

Ask opens questions prepared for that verse — guided, never an open chat.

Anywhere · hard to read?

Text size up to Elder, read aloud, and five language modes.

After step 6 · only if you want

Share a reflection publicly, or read with your group in Circles.

TabWhat's in itWhy it's a tab of its own
HomeContinue reading, jump to any book and chapter, recent booksOne tap back into the chapter — no decision about where to start (F3)
ReadSummary, verse-by-verse notes, questions, private reflectionThe whole core path on one scrolling page, like a sermon you can pause (F1, F2)
CommunityToday's reflection prompt and other readers' public reflectionsKept out of Read so the quiet learner never has to see it (F4)
CirclesPrivate church groups, shared reading plans, prayer topicsFor readers who do want a group — and the paid tier (F3)
ProfileLanguage, reading mode, text size, read aloud, privacySet once by a family member, then forgotten (persona 2)

04 · Designing the screens

Every screen answers something I heard.

The visual language is calm on purpose: warm paper, deep navy, one gold accent. Scripture is set in a serif and the explanations in a plain sans, so a reader can always tell the text from the note about it. Six of the screens, and the decision behind each:
